# Seasonal decorations

- Five seasons, judged by the date in Oslo: **Halloween**, 24 Oct to 1 Nov, one to three giant low-poly pumpkins near every stop (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-seasonal-decorations-design.md`), **Christmas**, the first Sunday of Advent (`firstAdventSunday`) through 6 January, one twinkling tree per stop (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-christmas-trees-design.md`), **17. mai**, 1 to 17 May, two fluttering Norwegian flags on every bus, coach and tram model (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-17-mai-flags-design.md`), **Pride**, all of June, the same flags in the classic six-stripe rainbow, and **New Year's fireworks**, 27 December to 1 January around the clock, over the Christmas trees (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-new-year-fireworks-design.md`). `seasonOn` (`src/domain/season.ts`) decides among Halloween, Christmas, 17. mai and Pride, and `fireworksOn` beside it whether the fireworks are on (see below); `?season=halloween`, `?season=christmas`, `?season=17mai` or `?season=pride` forces one on any date and `?season=none` forces all off. Read once per mount in `MapView`, so a kiosk left running keeps its trees past 6 January until it reloads; not part of `Filter`.
- The stop decorations share everything but their meshes and layers. A season's are a `DecorationSpec` in `DECORATIONS` (`src/domain/decorationPlacement.ts`): how many per stop, how tall, `aspect` (the footprint's widest point over the height — a tree's is its canopy, `TREE_ASPECT`), how far from the stop and how many spots to try. The trees stand 3–20 m out and try 48 spots against the pumpkins' 3–15 m and 24: a tree needs more room, and at the pumpkins' values about a third of a city centre's stops got one. Widen those, never `CLEARANCE_M` or `TRAFFIC_HALF_WIDTH_M`. Placement is pure: seeded from each stop's position, so a stop gets the same decorations on every load and pan; each decoration tries its spots against only the road segments and rings within reach of its stop, and the caller's `DecorationCache` keeps a stop's decorations between placements while the count of lines, rings and points near it is unchanged (the tiles hand out new objects every time, so identity says nothing). Roads have no width in the tiles, so clearance is from an estimated edge per `transportation` class (`TRAFFIC_HALF_WIDTH_M`), to the decoration's widest point; paths and cycleways are not traffic. Never inside a building. Obstacles are looked up through a grid clipped to the view: tilted, the loaded tiles reach the horizon, where a low-zoom line spans degrees, and an unclipped grid overflowed a `Map` and blanked the app. The pumpkins predate the sharing, and `decorationPlacement.test.ts`'s golden test pins every one over a fixed scene: a change to shared placement that moves a pumpkin fails it. A decoration's twinkle `phase` is hashed from its key, not drawn from the random stream, so adding it moved nothing. 17. mai and Pride have no entry: `DECORATIONS` `satisfies` a `Partial` record, and every reader goes through `decorationSpecFor`, which answers null for them, so `useSeasonalDecorations` starts no controller and no clock and costs nothing.
- `DecorationController` (plain TypeScript, tested with a fake map) reads stops, roads, buildings and water with `querySourceFeatures` (independent of the Transit network switch) and places them — in a city centre in 3D about 7 ms to read and 10–20 ms to place afresh, a couple once the cache holds — so only at start, on `moveend`, and for a base map tile arriving at rest, never mid-gesture; a terrain tile only re-reads heights. Whether anything changed is judged by each decoration's key, position and height: one that moves keeps its key. During a chase it places once a second (`DECORATION_CHASE_INTERVAL_MS`). Its cache is keyed by stop alone, so a controller serves one spec; another season is a new controller. Nothing is hidden around the chased vehicle: a 40 m clearance once did that, and took a stop's pumpkins away just as the bus arrived at it. Off the road a decoration can only cover the vehicle from a camera tilted near the horizon, briefly, and that is accepted. A placement that throws is logged and shows nothing: a decoration must never take the map down. `useSeasonalDecorations` keeps placing and drawing apart — the models' twenty-step fade, a scheme switch and a twinkle tick only redraw — and hands the layers to `VehicleModels`, which owns the one interleaved overlay. That overlay passes `deviceProps: { _reuseDevices: true }` (an underscore luma prop, typed in `DeviceProps`): StrictMode mounts it twice, two Decks race to attach to the map's one WebGL context and the second throws — remove it and `npm run dev` silently shows no 3D at all, while a production build, mounting once, is unaffected. Water is kept clear like buildings, and a ferry line counts as traffic with room for the boat.
- The meshes are in `vehicleMeshes.ts`. `pumpkinModel` (face in `LAMP_COLOURS`) is drawn by `pumpkinLayers` as three layers at most, the face only in dark mode. `christmasTreeModel` is a tiered canopy, a trunk, a star that reads as a star from any yaw, and bulbs in four colours spread evenly over the canopy in `TREE.bulbGroups` groups, every group carrying every colour so no colour pulses on its own. `christmasTreeLayers` draws all trees as `3 + TREE.bulbGroups` layers however many there are: the canopy tinted per tree, the trunk, the star, and one layer per bulb group so each group fades alone. Star and bulbs take the lamp material: drawn in both schemes, they glow only in dark mode. The star does not twinkle.
- **The twinkle** is a slow fade of each bulb group between full and `TWINKLE_MIN` over `TWINKLE_PERIOD_S` (3 s), offset by group and by the tree's `phase`, so neighbouring trees are out of step and nothing blinks. `twinkleBrightness` in `src/domain/twinkle.ts` is the curve's one definition; `TwinkleExtension` computes the same curve per vertex in GLSL, with `twinkle.ts`'s constants interpolated into the shader source, so a change to the formula is made in both. The phase is an instanced attribute, uploaded with the data; time, amount and group are uniforms, so a tick changes uniforms only — no placement, no mesh rebuild, no tile read, no attribute upload. The time is wrapped to one period in double precision before it becomes a float32 uniform, which on a weeks-long wall screen would step the fade. The extension is one shared instance: deck.gl compares extensions, and a new one per tick would rebuild the shaders.
- **The flags** — Norway's for 17. mai, the six-stripe rainbow for Pride — ride on the vehicles rather than standing at stops. `flagDesignFor(season)` (`season.ts`) names a season's `FlagDesign` (`norway`, `rainbow`) or null, and every flag reader asks it, never a season id, so another flag season is one entry there and one pattern in `FLAG_PATTERNS`. `FLAGGED_MODES` is bus, coach and tram, and only as a model with a usable bearing (`flaggedVehicles`): no flags on the 2D icons, the schedule ghost (so it stays visibly not the real vehicle), the directionless column (it has no front) or any other mode. They are drawn wherever the models are, so in 2D from `VEHICLE_MODEL_MIN_ZOOM` too, where from above a flag is nearly edge-on. `flagModelFor(mode, design)` (`vehicleMeshes.ts`, built once per mode and design, the poles one mesh per mode) is two poles and two cloths in model space, baked at `FLAG_EXAGGERATION` (3×) a real 30×20 cm flag on a 0.5 m pole; a test holds it equal to the stop poles' `STOP_POLE_EXAGGERATION`, so the two seasons' sense of scale is one number. The poles stand where the body's own profile says the roof is still flat — `flagMountFor` reads `cityBusSpec`, `coachSpec` and `tramSpec`, the same specs the hulls are built from, inside the rounded roof edge and behind the plan corner, the windscreen's rake or the tram's nose — so a change to a hull moves its flags with it rather than leaving them hovering beyond a raked front; the tests check each foot touches the body and the cloth flies above the roof and the destination signs. Every design shares the cloth's outline and `FLAG_COLUMNS`, so it hangs and bends alike; only the rows and the colours differ (`FLAG_PATTERNS`). The Nordic cross (`#BA0C2F`, white, `#00205B`) and the rainbow (`#E40303`, `#FF8C00`, `#FFED00`, `#008026`, `#004DFF`, `#750787`, six equal stripes, red at the top) are fixed vertex colours under a white `getColor` and the shaded material, so dark mode dims them like the body; they are not lamps. Its two faces share one plane, culled back-face, so each side shows only its own. Each cloth streams straight back along −y; a 20° outward splay was tried, so the chase camera behind would see more than an edge, and dropped because the flags looked better straight.
- `flagLayers` (`src/components/Seasonal/`) draws every flag as two layers per flagged mode in view, poles and cloth, with the models' `getPosition` and `modelOrientation` so they turn and lean with the vehicle. They are **kept apart from the model layers** — `useSeasonalFlags` hands them to `VehicleModels` through their own ref, called outside the effect that builds the models — so a flutter tick rebuilds only them and never the body, sign, detail, lamp or pool layers. A tick passes the **same array** of flagged vehicles every time (memoised per vehicle frame; `flaggedByMode` caches its split in a `WeakMap` keyed by it), so deck.gl sees unchanged `data` and keeps the instance attributes; only the cloth's uniforms change. The chased vehicle is drawn outside `vehicles`, rebuilt every chase frame, and takes its pose from `flutterNow`; `chasedFlagged` keeps the clock running when the chased vehicle is the only one flying flags, which otherwise would hang still for the whole chase. Its ticks then hand over nothing of their own: they call `onChasedTick`, which republishes the chased layers with the current pose unless a chase frame did within the tick — a chase skips frames while the vehicle stands still, and handing over an empty flag list instead re-sent every layer twelve times a second and left the chased flags frozen. It is asked of the vehicle the chase draws (`fliesFlags` on `chasedVehicleStore`, read with `useSyncExternalStore` as a boolean so a chase frame renders nothing), never of `data`: the chase goes on drawing its last report after the live cache drops the vehicle — routine for feeds reporting about once a minute — and derived from `data` the clock was disposed then, the flags froze, and the next report started a new clock at full flutter in one frame. **The flutter** is `flutterOffset` in `src/domain/flutter.ts`, the curve's one definition: a wave travelling back along the cloth over `FLUTTER_PERIOD_S`, its amplitude growing from the pole, on a per-vehicle `phase` hashed from `vehicleId`. `FlutterExtension` reproduces it per vertex with `flutter.ts`'s constants interpolated into the GLSL, reading the distance from the pole from the cloth's texture coordinates and displacing along model x through column 0 of the instance's model matrix, outward to each cloth's own side so the two flags mirror each other, with the side taken from the sign of model x — each cloth stays wholly on its own side, and a face and its back face share positions, so both move together, which the normal's sign would not give; it is one shared instance and the time is wrapped to one period in double precision, as for the twinkle. At `amount` 0 the cloth keeps a fixed gentle curve (`FLUTTER_STILL`), so a still flag still reads as cloth rather than a plate.
- `AnimationClock` (`src/components/Seasonal/animationClock.ts`) drives the lights and the flags, one instance per hook: the Christmas lights and the 17. mai and Pride flags. It ticks every 80 ms while what it animates is drawn — trees, or flagged vehicles in view or chased — and the page is visible. `useKioskInput`'s events and the map's `move` count as activity, so a chase keeps it running. After `ANIMATION_IDLE_MS` (60 s) of neither it eases to rest — lights steady and fully lit, flags in their still pose — over `ANIMATION_SETTLE_MS` (1 s), draws one last tick at amount 0 and stops its timer: a still map is then not repainted at all (measured with the vehicle feed closed: no `render` event in 10 s). Activity after the settle has begun eases back up over the same second from the amount showing (`animationWake`) — jumping straight to full dropped a dim bulb group by two thirds in one frame — and activity while active or easing up only defers the idle, so a chase's per-frame moves do not restart the ease. A tick that finds the page hidden stops the timer; the hook counts `visibilitychange` back to visible as activity, which starts it again. A clock can be handed an earlier clock's `startMs` and `lastActivityMs`; one whose last activity is past the idle time starts settled, with no timer until activity wakes it with the usual ease. The trees pass neither, so their clock is unchanged. `useSeasonalFlags` passes both: whether any flags are shown changes with the feed, not with anyone at the screen, so the hook watches input, `move` and `visibilitychange` all season, clock or none, and keeps the two times in a ref. A fresh clock for each flagged vehicle appearing on a still map once bought another 60 s of ticks — about 9 fps without a GPU on a fixed-view wall screen — and one created while someone is there now carries on rather than starting its time again. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` it never ticks, the lights are steady and the flags hang still; the preference is read when the clock starts. The amounts are pure, in `src/domain/animationAmount.ts`.
- **The fireworks** are not a season but an addition on top of one: the window lies inside Christmas, so `fireworksOn` (`season.ts`) is a second question beside `seasonOn`, and `?season=newyear` answers `christmas` there and `true` here — fireworks **and** trees, as the real dates would; `?season=christmas` keeps the trees alone. They are drawn only in 3D with the models shown (from `VEHICLE_MODEL_MIN_ZOOM`); otherwise `useSeasonalFireworks` runs no show, clock or timer, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` never does — a firework has no still pose. Scattered rockets go up every 2–4 s while someone is active; a **barrage** (24 rockets over 10 s) fires on every fifth minute of the wall clock, and the **finale** (72 over 60 s) replaces the 00:00 slot of 1 January, Oslo time (`fireworkSchedule.ts`). A slot's timing, colours and layout are seeded from its start, so every screen fires the same barrage, laid out over its own view; a missed slot is not fired late. Rockets go up over the ground under the **lower 60% of the screen** (`LAUNCH_SCREEN_SHARE`, less the map's bottom padding) and burst 60–120 m up: spread over the whole view's box, most burst above the top of the screen, a chase's nearly all. Each launch is its own screen point in that strip (`launchScreenPoint`), even on the screen rather than on the ground — tilted, an even spread over the ground crowds towards the horizon, and a chase's turned strip has a lng/lat box up to twice its size — and only that point is unprojected, when the rocket goes up; `placeLaunch` keeps it inside `cappedViewBounds`, or spreads it over the whole view when it cannot be placed. The show asks for that geometry (`LaunchView`, a function) only on a frame that launches: unprojecting raycasts the terrain on the CPU. `SPARK_SPEED` is tied to the lowest burst height by a test, so the drooping sparks end well above the models and stop poles. Two halves: `FireworkShow` (which rockets are launched, which bursts are still in the air, pruned in batches) and `FireworksClock` (when frames run: every frame while anything is in the air or a barrage runs, otherwise one timer — for the next scattered rocket while someone is there (`scatteredDueMs`), else for the next slot — so neither the seconds between scattered rockets nor an idle map between barrages are repainted). **Motion is in the shader**: `FireworksExtension` flies each rocket head and spark from numbers fixed at launch plus a time uniform, repeating `rocketRise`, `sparkOffset` and `sparkBrightness` in `src/domain/fireworks.ts` with its constants interpolated into the GLSL, so change both. Launch times are counted from an epoch reset whenever the instances are rebuilt (on a launch or a prune), keeping them small for float32; the rebuilt instances are a new array, which re-reads every accessor, and a frame changes the time uniform only. A slot's start is `slotStartMs`, compared to see whether the slot has changed before `slotAt` formats a date. The per-instance numbers are **packed into two vertex attributes**: `SimpleMeshLayer` uses 11, WebGL2 guarantees only 16, and one attribute each made 17 — the program failed to link and nothing was drawn, with only luma.gl's console error to show it. Unit tests cannot see a link failure; `fireworkLayers.test.ts` counts the declared attributes instead, so keep it passing when adding one, and the New Year test in `tests/seasonal.spec.ts` fails on any shader, link or luma.gl console error once rockets are up — its `rockets` count is what the show tracks, which rose while nothing could be drawn. A barrage is not activity: it calls `holdAwake` on the Christmas lights' clock for the barrage alone, so on an idle screen the lights twinkle with it and settle when it fades. Dark mode adds sparks to the map (`LIGHT_POOL_PARAMETERS`); light mode paints them over it, since additive light washes out on Positron; both fade by alpha alone and neither writes depth. `sparkModel` (`vehicleMeshes.ts`) is a white octahedron of `SPARK_RADIUS_M`, drawn once per rocket head and trail sample.
- Development builds expose `window.__seasonal = { season, count, twinkling }` for `tests/seasonal.spec.ts`; it replaces `__pumpkins`. The fireworks add `rockets`, the bursts in the air now; every writer spreads the previous value, so none drops another's fields. For the trees, `twinkling` says whether the clock runs with motion allowed, and stays true once it has settled. In the flag season `useSeasonalDecorations` leaves the hook alone and `useSeasonalFlags` writes it: `count` is the flagged vehicles drawn, the chased one included, and `twinkling` whether the flags are moving now, false once settled. `tests/seasonal.spec.ts` opens the flag tests in 2D at Stavanger byterminal and presses "Show 3D view": loaded cold with `?view=3d`, the whole country's terrain keeps software WebGL from reaching the map's `load` within the test. The flags depend on the live feed, so the test needs a bus with a bearing near the terminal; Oslo bussterminal, where it first stood, had almost none in the measurement, while Stavanger had 11–14 flagged.

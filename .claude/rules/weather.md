# Weather

- The "Weather" switch in the Layers panel (off by default, `MapView` state like the ghost vehicle's, both modes) draws rain or snow over the map **in 3D only, and only from `VEHICLE_MODEL_MIN_ZOOM`**, where vehicles are 3D models — further out nothing is drawn or fetched. The switch is disabled in 2D and keeps its setting. It is not in the URL and not part of the kiosk's setup, so a kiosk link starts with it off.
- It is two things from one reading (`WeatherFeed`, inside `<Map>`, reports it to `MapView`): **precipitation**, a 2D canvas over the map (`WeatherOverlay`), and the **sky** — `skyFor(scheme, weather)` in `src/domain/baseMapScheme.ts`, written by `BaseMapScheme`, which stays the sky's only writer and takes the weather as a prop. The sky greys with the cloud cover, darkens in rain, stays paler in snow, and hazes the far terrain in its horizon colour; off, unknown or clear it is the scheme's own sky, the same object, so nothing is rewritten. The sky and haze only show from about 70° of pitch, where the horizon comes on screen (the view tilts to 80°); at the default 55° this changes nothing. MapLibre's fog reaches terrain only, **not building extrusions**, so MET's `fog` reads as a pale overcast rather than fog — measured, distant buildings stay solid. A tint over the map was rejected because it dims the data colours; do not add one to make fog convincing.
- The canvas is in screen space, **above** the vehicles and their labels and below every control and panel (`zIndex: 1`), and takes no input. Particles lean with the wind's speed but always the same way on screen, whatever the map's bearing. No particles are drawn for `prefers-reduced-motion`; the sky still follows the weather, since a colour is not motion.
- The data is MET Norway's Nowcast for the map's centre (`weather-nowcast-endpoint` in bootstrap.json), called **straight from the browser**. MET's terms allow that for low-volume sites and identify the page by its `Origin`; they do not answer CORS preflights, so the request must stay "simple" — no custom headers — and coordinates are cut to four decimals (`nowcastUrl`). Heavy traffic would need a caching proxy.
- `weatherFromNowcast` (`src/domain/weather.ts`) reads the rate now for whether anything falls and the coming hour's symbol for snow or not and, while nothing falls, for the cloud cover (`clear`, `partly`, `cloudy`, `fog`) — the Nowcast measures no cloud, so the cover is a forecast where the rate is a measurement, and whatever falls is drawn under an overcast sky regardless of the symbol; a symbol it does not know leaves the sky clear; outside radar coverage it is null and nothing is drawn. `useWeather` refetches when `needsRefetch` says so — five minutes, or the centre more than 5 km away — asked on `moveend` and a 30 s timer, so a chase costs a comparison per frame, not a request.
- The credit is not a map source's, so `MapAttribution` takes it as `credit` and remounts the control when it changes: MapLibre reads `customAttribution` once. The strip is then a new element, so its height is read from whichever strip is in the bottom-right corner when the corner resizes, never from one strip held on to: the old strip is still in the document when the effect runs, and measuring it reported 0 once it was removed, which sank the phone's sheet over the credit.
- The reading's numbers are shown by `WeatherCard`, in the bottom-right corner above the attribution strip, whenever the weather is drawn. `weatherDetailsFromNowcast` (`src/domain/weatherDetails.ts`) is separate from `weatherFromNowcast` because it still answers outside radar coverage, where temperature and wind are published and the card says "Not measured here" for the rate. `useWeather` returns both from one response. A 422 — a place outside the Nowcast's area — clears the reading rather than keeping the last one, which would be for somewhere the map has left.
- The card follows MET's terms, and each part is there for one of them: values as delivered with units (CC BY asks that changes be indicated; there are none), the coming hour's symbol labelled "Next hour" because it is a forecast, "Data from MET Norway" with a link (their suggested wording), and the time the reading was fetched (which they ask for). No Yr logo, name or weather symbols: the terms forbid a service that looks like theirs. A symbol code `symbolLabel` does not know is left out, never shown raw. The licence link stays in the strip.
- On a phone the card is one line and stands above the sheet. It is hidden with the sheet `full` and during a chase, where above the HUD it stood on the chased vehicle. Wider, it lifts above the chase HUD — centred, but as wide as its text, and under about 1100px it reached the corner and covered the credit; lifted at every width rather than by a guess at the HUD's — and above the kiosk's band, and is hidden while a detail card is open on a screen under 760px, too narrow for the two side by side. In a kiosk run it follows the same rules, so it shows on a wall screen and not in a phone's chase: it is data, not a control, and is not hidden with them. It is rendered before the detail panels, which share its z-index, and eases its `bottom` like the sheet, so a sheet dragged up covers it instead of passing behind it.

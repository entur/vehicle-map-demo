# Vehicle models and destination signs

- From `VEHICLE_MODEL_MIN_ZOOM` (16) vehicle icons cross-fade into true-scale 3D models, in both 2D and 3D view. The models are **built in code** (`src/domain/vehicleMeshes.ts`), not loaded from glTF: no licence-clean model pack covers the Norwegian fleet (metro, coach and ferry especially), and building them makes every model match `VEHICLE_DIMENSIONS` in `vehicleFootprint.ts` by construction — the tests hold the two together. deck.gl (`SimpleMeshLayer` in an interleaved `MapLibreOverlay`, `VehicleModels.tsx`) renders them, so buildings and terrain can hide them; that occlusion is accepted. That needs deck.gl's layer group in the style, which `MapLibreOverlay` adds only while `map.isStyleLoaded()` is true — and vehicle frames keep it false, so outside a chase the group was never added and the models were drawn over the finished map. Every handover of layers therefore goes through `withLayerAsStyleLoaded` (`src/utils/`), which answers `isStyleLoaded()` with whether `vehicle-layer` exists for the length of that synchronous call. Model space is +y forward, so the layer's yaw is `-bearing` (deck.gl yaw turns counter-clockwise). A vehicle without a usable bearing is drawn as a directionless column rather than a model pointing north. Each model is four meshes: a pure-white **body** whose colour comes entirely from the per-vehicle `getColor` (`paintFor` in `src/domain/vehiclePaint.ts`: the line's published `line.presentation.colour` when there is one, otherwise the mode colour), destination **signs**, **details** (glass, wheels, underframe) and **lamps** (head, tail, indicator and the ferry's navigation lights), the last two in fixed vertex colours drawn with a white `getColor`. deck.gl multiplies `getColor` into vertex colours, so tinting a single mesh would tint headlights too, and the layer's default colour is black. The builder routes a primitive to the body or the signs only when passed the `PAINT` or `SIGN` marker, each compared by identity, and to the lamps when its colour is one of `LAMP_COLOURS`, also by identity — a new lamp colour must be added there. The lamps are their own mesh so their layer can take its own `material`. `MODEL_MATERIALS` in `vehicleModelLayers.ts` lights every model layer by the colour scheme: in light mode all alike; in dark mode the body, details and directionless column take `DARK_SHADING` (0.7) of the usual light, so the model reads as night, while the lamps take ambient light only at 1.6× — the taillights' own colour is darker than a red body, and clipping turns amber indicators more yellow — and the signs ambient light only at exactly 1×, a lit display whose text stays as drawn. The body's colour is the line's, which is data, so do not dim it much further against Fiord. In dark mode every mode but the ferry also casts a **light pool** on the road ahead (`src/domain/lightPool.ts`): a flat quad from the vehicle's front, at its own height plus `LIGHT_POOL_LIFT`, whose soft wedge is all texture alpha (`lightPoolTexture.ts`), since `SimpleMeshLayer`'s vertex colours carry none. It is blended additively without writing depth and drawn before the models. Flat, it vanished into any uphill street, lift or not, so models and pools **lean with the terrain**: `vehicleGround` (`src/domain/vehicleGround.ts`) takes four height lookups per report — position, rear, front, and the pool's far end — and gives the body the slope from rear to front and the pool its own slope from the position (its pivot) to its far end, since the road ahead can rise more than the road underneath. The slope goes into deck.gl's **roll**, the third orientation angle: with the model along +y, roll is what lifts the nose. Results are cached per report only when every sample was found, so an unloaded tile is retried on the next frame; a missing slope sample leaves that part level. The lift still covers a road that bends within the pool's length. `VehicleModels` reads the scheme with `mapSchemeFor`, as `BaseMapScheme` does. Road and rail vehicles carry a sign at each end and on each side, so one is in view from any angle; the ferry has none, and gets no sign layer. Height comes from `map.queryTerrainElevation`, not deck.gl's experimental `TerrainExtension`.
- The signs show the vehicle's **`destinationName`**, as published, lit in `signColourFor` (the published `textColour`, otherwise a default amber) on a dark display; with no destination the display is blank. In `SimpleMeshLayer` a texture **replaces** the colour rather than being tinted by `getColor`, so the colour is drawn into the texture along with the text (`signTexture.ts`, a canvas cached by text and colour), and since a layer takes one texture, signs are drawn as **one layer per destination and colour** (`signGroups` in `src/domain/destinationSign.ts`). The sign mesh's texture coordinates come from `signUVs` in the builder: every sign face shows the whole texture upright, readable from outside and at `SIGN_TEXTURE_ASPECT`, as large as fits — the rest clamps to the texture's blank edge. The tests check the reading direction per face. The text is legible only from about zoom 20. `vehicleModelLayers` builds **no layers at all while the models are hidden** (below `VEHICLE_MODEL_MIN_ZOOM`): zoomed out, the whole feed is in the subscription, and on dev its signs alone came to 1,672 layers, each with a texture to draw and upload — measured, 1.9 fps against 25. Zoomed in, the subscription's bbox keeps the groups few — and `VehicleModels` also builds models only for vehicles in view (`vehiclesInView`, read on `moveend`, skipped during a chase), because straight after a jump from the whole country to street level the data is still the country until the new subscription's first frame: measured, 1,097 layers and a frozen page for about 4 s; the texture cache (256) would thrash if a view ever held more. Measured on dev, 3,482 of 5,709 vehicles carry a destination; rail (VYG, FLT, GOA), the coaches, SKA and VAR publish none, so their signs are dark.
- The selected journey's **stop poles** reuse this machinery: `stopPoleModel` (body = board edges, painted `ROUTE` or `SEVERITY_SEVERE`; sign = the board's two faces; details = the pole; no lamps) and `signTexture` with a background argument, the name in ink or white on the board's colour, whichever contrasts more (`mostLegibleOn` in `contrast.ts`). One name layer per call, since a layer takes one texture.
